## The challenge

Big social platforms weren't built with African identity as the starting point. The product brief describes iAfricano as the opposite: "a purpose-built space where being African is the default, not an afterthought." It is for Africans on the continent who want to share their culture, and for people in the diaspora who want to stay close to their roots.

My job was to turn the client's product requirements document into one coherent mobile app: spotting the gaps in the document and settling the design decisions as the product took shape.

## My role

I owned the app design, from reading the requirements to preparing the files for the developer. I set up the design tokens in Tokens Studio and designed onboarding, sign-up, login and password recovery, the home feed and posts, Explore, communities, collections, profiles, chats, notifications and settings.

Alongside the screens, I built a reference library for patterns that repeat across the app, and interactive HTML mockups to click through the flows.

## Key decisions

- **Make heritage visible.** In an app built around cultural identity, heritage shouldn't be hidden in settings. People pick the countries that make up their heritage and the languages they speak. Both show on their profile as flags and tags, and a small country tag sits next to their name on posts and in chats, so identity is part of every interaction, not a profile detail.
- **Keep the home feed focused.** The first idea was a row of country filters on the home feed. With 54 African countries, that row would have been crowded and hard to use. I moved browsing by country to Explore, so the feed stays about content and country discovery gets its own place.
- **Share with people who share your heritage.** The requirements listed two post audiences: Public and People I follow. While designing the post composer I added a third: people who share your heritage. It gives people another way to choose who sees a post, built on the connection the whole product is about.
- **One library for repeating patterns.** Across 40+ screens, the same menus, sheets and dialogs would easily drift apart if each was drawn separately. I built a library of 24 numbered patterns, from post menus to delete confirmations, kept apart from the screens so each is designed once, reused, and gives the developer one reference to build from.
